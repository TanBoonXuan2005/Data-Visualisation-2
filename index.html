Australian Heritage Sites

Exploring where Australia's nationally significant heritage places are located, what they represent, and why their preservation matters.

New South Wales and Victoria lead with 30 places each. Together they hold 59 of the 126 listed places, close to half.

What makes these places significant?

More than half of Australia's World Heritage properties (12 of 21) are listed for their natural values alone. Only five are purely cultural, and four are recognised for both.

Earth's history (criterion viii) is the most common reason for listing, applying to 12 of the 21 properties. No cultural criterion applies to more than four.

Criteria i to vi are cultural: human creativity, exchange of ideas, cultural traditions, buildings and landscapes, traditional land use, and links to beliefs or events. Criteria vii to x are natural: exceptional beauty, Earth's history, living ecosystems and biodiversity.

From 1981 to 2003, every Australian World Heritage property was natural or mixed. Five of the six inscribed since 2004 are cultural, including two Aboriginal cultural landscapes, Budj Bim (2019) and Murujuga (2025). Recognition can also change after inscription: Kakadu was extended in 1987 and 1992, and Uluṟu-Kata Tjuṯa was recognised for its cultural values in 1994.

Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Additional dates reflect the selected UNESCO dataset fields and do not represent a complete history of all decisions.
